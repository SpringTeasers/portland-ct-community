Portland, Connecticut

About Portland

Portland is a town in Middlesex County, Connecticut, located on the east bank of the Connecticut River, across the Arrigoni Bridge from Middletown. Incorporated in 1841, it is a residential community with active businesses and a dedicated industrial park.

How the town is run

Government

The Town of Portland’s chief executive official is the First Selectman. The First Selectman is elected by the people every two years in odd-numbered years, along with the six-member Board of Selectmen, which is responsible for fiscal management.

Town meetings are held to decide special issues. In accordance with the Portland Town Charter, the Annual Town Budget Meeting is held on the first Monday in the month of May, and the presented budget is then submitted to the voters at a Budget Referendum held the following Monday. Property tax is the primary source of revenue.

The original Charter of the Town of Portland was approved in 1982 and implemented in 1983. It was amended in 1993, 1995, 1997, 2000, and 2004.

Source: Town of Portland, Government — portlandct.org/town-government

History

History of Portland

Early settlement

The original inhabitants of the area now called Portland belonged to a Native American tribe known as the Wangunk — the name refers to the “big bend” of the Connecticut River, which curves around half the town’s perimeter. The first European settlers came to the Portland area in the 1650s; the first was James Stanclift, an English stonecutter. They were drawn by the availability of brownstone, a valuable resource for construction and for gravestones. The proximity of the river to the brownstone quarries made transporting cut stone affordable, and a number of flourishing family businesses developed.

The brownstone industry

Portland supplied quality brownstone to New York and Boston and as far as San Francisco, and employed over 1,500 people during the 1850s. More than 25 ships transported the stone to major population centers in the United States, Canada, and England. Local businesses tied to the quarry industry included shipbuilding, transportation, animal husbandry, blacksmithing, and mill-work. The quarries’ need for labor brought waves of immigrants to Portland — first the Irish, then the Swedes, and to a lesser extent Italians.

The market for brownstone declined at the turn of the 20th century, as brownstone could not compete with less expensive and more versatile materials such as concrete. In 1936, the river flooded into the quarry sites, effectively ending the brownstone industry. The scenic quarries, located just off Main Street in the central business district, are recognized as historically significant today.

From East Middletown to Portland

The town was first known as East Middletown, maintaining its ties across the river. It became incorporated as Chatham in 1767, which included what is now known as East Hampton and Middle Haddam. When these three districts gained distinct characters of their own, separation again occurred, each taking its present-day name. The name Portland was borrowed from Portland in the English county of Dorset, which was famous for its quarry industry.

Source: Town of Portland, History — portlandct.org/history

Getting here

Location & geography

Portland is located in Middlesex County in central Connecticut, on the east bank of the Connecticut River, across the Arrigoni Bridge from Middletown. The town offers the convenience of central Connecticut — midway between Boston and New York — with access to shopping malls, cultural programs, airports, and the capital city of Hartford.

Sources: Town of Portland — Our Community and Visitors Information. Details of highways, airports and transit are on the Town Services & Links page.

Everyday life

A community in the truest sense

To its residents, the Town of Portland is one of the best-kept secrets in New England. This quiet Yankee village, somewhat removed from the rest of the world by a sharp curve in the Connecticut River, maintains its nineteenth-century appeal while offering quality, up-to-date services. Portland is a village centered on families, friendships, and old-time traditions: neighbors chat at local shops, town meetings invite participation, and the town’s Main Street provides a setting for walking, jogging, and bicycle rides.

Portland is home to nationally acclaimed schools, active churches, responsive public works, full-time police services, and a modern library. Community amenities include a modern Senior Center, an expanding Parks & Recreation department, special programs for kids sponsored by Youth Services, quality golf courses, riverfront marinas, and annual events such as the 4th of July fireworks display, parades, festivals, fairs, and concerts.

Source: Town of Portland, Our Community — portlandct.org/our-community
